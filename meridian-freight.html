The Challenge

Three products, three different teams, six years of independent decisions. The carrier dashboard used one status-color system, the broker tool used another, and the shipper portal had invented a third — so an operations manager working across all three had to relearn what ‘yellow’ meant every time they switched tabs.

Support tickets were climbing, and half of them weren’t bugs — they were people who couldn’t find a number that was on the screen, just styled differently than they expected. Ops teams had started building their own spreadsheets to route around the software entirely.

Approach

I audited all three products screen by screen — over 40 in total — and ran contextual inquiry sessions at a partner warehouse to watch dispatchers actually work, not describe how they worked. The gap between the two was the whole project: people were scanning for exceptions, not reading dashboards top to bottom, and none of the three products were designed for that.

Process

8 months · 2023
01

Audit

Catalogued 40+ screens across all three products to find every place the same information was represented differently.

02

Unify

Built a shared token and component library — not a new visual style, a new source of truth for status, density, and type.

03

Prototype

Designed a high-density data table pattern and a status system that used shape and icon, not color alone, to flag urgency.

04

Pilot

Rolled the new dashboard out to a single carrier team first, instrumented heavily, before touching the other two products.

05

Scale

Extended the system to broker and shipper surfaces once the pilot’s numbers held up, with four product teams now building on the same components.

Solution

The redesign wasn’t a new look — it was a shared vocabulary. A status could now be read by shape as well as color, so a colorblind dispatcher and a hurried one saw the same urgency at a glance. The load-tracking dashboard, the most-used screen by volume, dropped from an 11-click task to 4.

Underneath, a token-based component library meant the three teams stopped rebuilding the same table pattern three different ways — which mattered more for velocity than any single screen redesign.

Task completion on the core load-tracking flow dropped from 6.2 minutes to 2.1, and support tickets tied to ‘can’t find X’ issues fell by 38% within the first two quarters. The component library built for this project is now the default starting point for new enterprise features across the org.

Design decision

We defined status by shape and icon first, color second — color carried the accent brand identity elsewhere in the product, and reusing it for semantic urgency would have meant the two systems eventually collided.
