Canopy

Redesigning the digital home of a wildlife conservation NGO — from a dated microsite into a donation engine that carries the urgency of the mission.

The Challenge

The organization’s site hadn’t changed since 2014. It photographed well in board meetings and converted almost no one — donors landed on the homepage, skimmed a wall of text, and left before they understood why this particular forest mattered more than any other cause in their inbox.

Analytics told the quieter story: 68% of sessions ended within 20 seconds, and the donation form — six fields deep, with no indication of impact — completed at under 2%. The brand itself had drifted too: print materials used one palette, the website another, and neither matched the photography the field teams were actually producing.

Approach

I started with twelve donor interviews and a teardown of the existing funnel, then sat with the field team to understand what made their work legible to an outsider — which turned out to be almost none of what was on the site. A competitive audit of five conservation orgs showed a pattern: the ones that converted led with a specific animal, a specific place, and a number, not a mission statement.

That became the brief: replace abstraction with specificity, and rebuild the visual system around real photography instead of stock.

Process

5 months · 2024
01

Discover

12 donor interviews plus funnel analytics surfaced where urgency was getting lost between the homepage and the donation form.

02

Define

Donor personas and three narrative pillars — place, species, proof — gave the content team a shared brief instead of a style guide nobody read.

03

Design system

New type, a canopy-toned palette, custom iconography per region, and a photography direction that replaced stock with field footage.

04

Build & test

Five rounds of usability testing on the donation flow alone, cutting it from six fields to three before a single line of production code shipped.

05

Launch & measure

Phased rollout by region, with the donation funnel instrumented from day one so the redesign’s impact was never a guess.

Solution

The rebuild centered on one idea: show, don’t summarize. Each region got its own landing narrative built from real photography, a short video loop, and a single clear ask. The donation flow dropped from six fields to three, with optional information deferred to a post-donation email instead of gatekeeping the gift itself.

The visual identity followed the same logic — a custom iconography set standing in for the stock leaf-and-globe clip art conservation sites default to, and a palette pulled from actual canopy photography rather than a mood board.

Outcome

+128%Donor conversion
+64%Time on site
−41%Bounce rate

Within the first full quarter post-launch, donor conversion more than doubled and the redesigned visual system was adopted as the brand standard across three regional chapters — replacing years of inconsistent print and digital assets with one system everyone could actually use.

Design decision

We moved four of the seven donation-form fields to an optional post-gift email instead of the checkout step itself — legal needed the data eventually, not immediately, and the three-second decision to give shouldn’t have to wait on it.
