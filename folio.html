The Challenge

The product connected retail investors to independent publishing houses — a genuinely interesting idea buried under a nine-step KYC signup that looked like it belonged to a mortgage application. Over 60% of people who started onboarding never finished it.

The brand made it worse: a palette and type system borrowed wholesale from generic fintech, which alienated exactly the audience the product needed — people who cared enough about independent publishing to invest in it, and expected the product to look like it understood that.

Approach

I mapped every field in the signup against what compliance actually required versus what the team had simply assumed was necessary — the gap between those two lists was bigger than anyone expected. In parallel, a brand audit compared fintech conventions against the aesthetic of the publishing world itself: typography, paper texture, the visual language of a bookshop rather than a brokerage.

Process

6 months · 2025
01

Map constraints

Sat with the compliance partner to separate legally required fields from assumed ones — nearly half the form fell into the second category.

02

Redesign flow

Rebuilt onboarding as three steps with progressive disclosure, deferring anything non-blocking to after account creation.

03

Brand system

A warmer palette, editorial type, and a small illustration set replaced the generic fintech visual language.

04

Test & iterate

Three rounds of moderated usability testing against the new flow, catching two points of confusion the compliance language itself was causing.

05

Launch

Phased rollout with drop-off instrumented at every step of the new flow, not just at the end.

Solution

The nine-step form became three: identity, funding, and preferences — with two previously mandatory-looking disclosures moved to an optional post-signup step once legal confirmed they weren’t blocking requirements. Progressive disclosure did the rest, showing only the fields relevant to what someone had just told the product about themselves.

The brand refresh ran in parallel rather than after — editorial type pairings, a warmer palette pulled from book cloth and paper stock, and a small illustration set replacing the stock icon language most fintech products default to.

Onboarding completion more than doubled and NPS climbed 22 points in the two quarters following launch — with qualitative feedback shifting from ‘felt like a bank’ to specific mentions of the illustration system and tone of voice.

Design decision

We moved two disclosures that felt mandatory but legally weren’t to an optional step after account creation, with sign-off from compliance — shortening the critical path without cutting anything required.
